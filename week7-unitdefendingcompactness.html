Session Concepts — Introduce Before Warm-Up
H
Concept 1
Horizontal Compactness
The defensive unit stays narrow — no gaps between players across the width of the pitch. When the ball moves wide, the whole unit shifts as one. When the ball is central, the unit squeezes in. The shape of the unit moves like a single object, not 4–5 individuals. Gaps between players across the width = space for attackers to receive and turn between the lines.
"No daylight between you — shift as one."
V
Concept 2
Vertical Compactness
The defensive and midfield lines stay close in depth — no gap between the two lines. The classic breakdown: midfield presses high while the back line drops deep, creating 15–20m of open space between them. Attackers receive in this pocket and have time to turn and play. Vertical compactness eliminates that pocket entirely.
"No gap between the lines — squeeze the space."

Warm-Up — Compactness Rondo
Warm-Up · All 22 · Introduce Concepts
Connected Rondo — Compactness Constraint
4 groups of 4 · phase 1: 3v1 box · phase 2: 5v2 connected pair
15MIN
Objective
Introduce both compactness concepts physically before players need to apply them tactically. The rondo constraints do the coaching — defenders who don't stay compact lose the ball immediately. No lecture needed. By the end of Phase 2, players have a felt sense of what horizontal compactness means in their bodies.
Phase 1 — 3v1 Box Rondo (6 min)
1.
Groups of 4. Three passers, one defender. Defender must stay within a 4×4m box — they cannot step outside it to intercept. The box is the horizontal compactness constraint made physical.
2.
If the defender leaves the box, the point goes to the passers. Defender earns a point for a clean interception or forced error inside the box.
3.
Rotate the defender every 90 seconds. After all four have defended, ask: "What did staying in the box force you to do?" Answer: stay connected, don't chase.
Phase 2 — 5v2 Connected Pair (8 min)
1.
5 passers, 2 defenders. The two defenders must stay within 3m of each other at all times — they are connected. If the gap between them exceeds 3m, passers earn a free point.
2.
The pair must move as a unit — when one shifts left, the other shifts left. One presses, the other covers. This is horizontal compactness in a 2-player unit.
3.
Defenders earn a point for intercepting or winning the ball. Rotate pairs every 90 seconds.
4.
Key question after: "When the pair moved together — was it harder or easier to break through them?"
Coaching Points
The space between defenders is the problem — not the defenders themselves
When a passer finds a gap between the two defenders, ask: "whose job was that gap?" The answer is both of theirs. The gap is shared responsibility. This is the shift from individual defending to unit defending — the concept the whole session is built around.
One presses, one covers — never both press
When one defender in the pair steps to press, the other must hold a covering position. Two defenders pressing simultaneously = no cover = gap. The pressing trigger is when the ball-carrier's head is down or they take a heavy touch. Until then, one presses, one holds.

Bridge — Warm-Up to P1 · 60 seconds
Ask: "In the rondo — when the pair moved together, was it easier to score or harder?" Take two answers. Then: "That's exactly what we're building now — but with a full defensive unit. Horizontal: stay narrow, no gaps across the width. Vertical: no gap between your defensive line and your midfield. Two lines, one unit."
Practice 1 — Shape & Shift Grid
Shape Work · Split Groups · Simultaneous
Zoned Grid — Horizontal Shift + Vertical Compactness
Group A: ball-side unit shift · Group B: mid-def gap live 5v5 · 25 min
25MIN
Group A — Coach 1 · Horizontal Shift (Shadow + Live)
1.
Pitch divided into 3 vertical zones (left, centre, right) with cones. Defending unit of 4 must always keep the majority of players in the zone where the ball is — they shift as a unit when the ball moves.
2.
Shadow phase (8 min): coach calls "left!", "centre!", "right!" and the unit shifts. No ball. Players must move before the next call. Gap between any two defenders wider than 4m = coach stops and resets.
3.
Live phase (12 min): 4v4+1 (neutral). Attacking team score by passing into a wide zone that has no defender in it. Defending team shifts in response to every pass. Defending team earn a point for every 10 consecutive passes they survive without a gap being exploited.
4.
Key cue: "ball goes left — everyone shifts left. Nearest player presses, rest hold the shape."
Group B — Coach 2 · Vertical Compactness (Live 5v5)
1.
Pitch divided into 3 horizontal zones (defensive, middle, attacking). A "forbidden zone" is marked in the middle channel — if the attacking team receives and turns in this zone with no pressure on them, they score a free point. This is the gap between defensive and midfield lines made visible.
2.
5v5 live. Both teams attack and defend. The defending team must keep their two lines (2 defenders + 2 mids) within 10m of each other at all times — no splitting of the lines.
3.
If a player receives freely in the middle forbidden zone: play stops, coach asks "who let the gap open — the defenders or the midfielders?" Answer is always: both. The gap is shared.
4.
Key cue: "midfield and defence — same block. When defence drops, midfield drops. No space between you."
Objective
Group A builds the habit of horizontal shifting as a unit — the whole line moves when the ball moves, with no individual stepping out independently. Group B makes the vertical compactness concept competitive — the forbidden zone in the middle makes the consequence of a gap immediately visible and painful. Both groups develop the same core idea: the unit moves and defends together, or it doesn't work at all.
Horizontal Compactness — Group A Key Idea
The unit's width is determined by the ball's position. Central ball = unit narrows. Wide ball = unit shifts but still stays connected — the far-side defender does not stay in their zone, they follow the shift. The first defender presses the ball-carrier; the unit contracts behind them, covering the gaps. Nobody defends alone.
Vertical Compactness — Group B Key Idea
The two lines (midfield and defence) operate as a single block. When the ball is high and the midfield presses, the defensive line must hold a position close behind them — not drop deep to the goal-line. When defenders drop, midfield must drop with them. The block moves together. The pocket between the lines is where most goals were conceded — eliminating it removes the most dangerous space on the pitch.
Coaching Points — Both Groups
Call the shift — don't expect telepathy
The unit only shifts together if players communicate. The player nearest the ball calls "right!" or "left!" as they press. The rest respond immediately. Silent defending produces individual reactions. Noisy defending produces unit reactions. Cue: "the first press must come with a call."
Communication
Nobody holds their position while everyone else shifts
The most common breakdown: ball goes wide, three defenders shift, one stays central "holding their position." Now there's a 6m gap between player three and player four. There is no individual position in unit defending — there is only a unit position. When the unit shifts, everyone shifts.
Horizontal
The gap between lines is shared — not the back four's problem alone
When an attacker receives in the middle zone, it is tempting to say the defenders dropped too deep. It is equally possible the midfielders pressed too high. The gap between lines is always a shared failure. Debrief this specifically — ask both lines what they could have done differently.
Vertical
Progressions
A+
Group A: add a second ball — coach plays a second ball into a different zone as soon as the first ball is played. Unit must re-shift immediately. Tests speed and communication of the horizontal shift.
B+
Group B: narrow the forbidden zone to 6m — smaller target for attackers, more precise vertical compactness required from defenders.
Regressions
A−
Stay in shadow phase only — coach calls the zone, unit shifts without live opposition. Establish the movement habit before adding competitive pressure.
B−
Widen the forbidden zone to 15m — makes it easier to avoid and allows players to understand the concept before the constraint tightens.

Practice 2 — 8v8 + GK Unit Defending Game
SSG · All 22 · Unit Defending Conditions
8v8 + GK — Shape Rewarded, Gaps Punished
Quarter pitch · 2 conditions · unit defending earns free ball · central gap goal = +2
25MIN
Objective
8v8 + GK on the full quarter pitch. The two conditions reflect both unit defending concepts from P1 directly into a game. Defending well earns a tangible reward — a free ball restart. Conceding from the central zone (the vertical gap) earns the attacking team double. Both conditions create competitive pressure to apply the session's principles rather than reverting to individual defending habits. Final 5 minutes open — see what is retained naturally.

Rules
1.
Normal football. Two cone lines mark the central zone between the defensive and midfield lines (same zone as P1 Group B).
2.
If any player receives the ball in this zone with no pressure on them, the attacking team earn +2 if they score from that possession.
3.
When the defending team wins the ball: coach quickly checks whether all outfield players are in their correct horizontal zone. If yes — coach calls "shape!" and the team gets a free restart. This rewards collective defensive organisation.
4.
When the ball goes wide: the coach may call "shift!" as a prompt — the team must respond immediately. If they don't, the attacking team earn a free pass into the now-uncovered zone.
5.
Final 5 minutes: no conditions, open play. Watch for natural retention of shape habits.
Coaching Points — Debrief After P2
How many times did you earn the free ball? What did that feel like?
Players who experienced the "shape!" reward will remember the feeling of being organised. Ask them to identify which moment they felt the unit was most compact — and what allowed it. Build the positive reference point before the next match.
When the +2 goal went in — which line was responsible?
Revisit the shared responsibility concept from P1. The +2 goal is a visual moment where the vertical gap was exploited. Ask both the defenders and midfielders: "where were you when that pass went in?" They identify the failure themselves — more durable than being told.
Final 5 minutes — did the shape hold without the rule?
This is the diagnostic question. If players were still shifting as a unit and holding vertical compactness in the open final 5 minutes, the habit is beginning to form. If not — name it honestly: "that's what we're building. It won't be automatic after one session. But now you know what it looks like."
Progression
+
GK must initiate horizontal shifts verbally — calling "left!" or "right!" as the ball moves. If GK is silent when the ball moves zones, attacking team earn a free pass. Develops GK as unit organiser.
If Shape Keeps Collapsing
−
Brief freeze every time the central zone is exploited — physically reorganise the defending unit in slow motion. Show the correct block position, restart. Max 3 freezes before removing the condition entirely for 5 minutes and letting them play freely, then reintroduce.
