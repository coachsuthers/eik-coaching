1
Principle One
Delay
Close at pace — arrive under control. The first defender's job is to slow the attack and buy time for the unit to set, not to win the ball immediately. Rushing the ball-carrier or the long ball creates gaps that are immediately exploitable.
Foundation of P1 · Applied in P2 & P3
2
Principle Two
Restraint
Hold the line — don't step to the ball. A defender who steps forward to a long ball early leaves space in behind. Restraint means reading the flight, getting goal-side, and letting the ball come to the defender rather than chasing it. Patience is a defensive weapon.
Core of P1 High Ball · Maintained in P2 & P3
3
Principle Three
Shape Before Press
The unit must be organised before any individual engages. One defender pressing while three others are out of position is worse than no press at all. The trigger to press only fires when the unit is set and a clear advantage exists. Uncoordinated pressing is how 10 goals happen.

Practice 1 — Functional: Delay & High Ball Control
Functional · Split · Simultaneous
1v1 Delay Channel + High Ball Body Shape
Group A & B run simultaneously · 11 each · same half of the quarter pitch
20MIN
Group A — Coach 1 · 1v1 Delay Channel
1.
Set up 5 channels (cones), ~8m long. Attacker starts at one end with the ball. Defender starts at the other end.
2.
Defender must delay the attacker for a full 5 seconds before they are allowed to attempt a tackle. Diving in early = automatic point to attacker.
3.
Attacker scores by reaching the end line. Defender scores by winning the ball after 5 seconds of delay.
4.
Rotate after each rep. Coach calls the 5-second count aloud: "one… two… three… four… five — now!"
The point of the 5-second rule: it physically forces the defender to experience what delay feels like. The instinct to lunge is removed by the rule — and players discover that jockeying for 5 seconds is actually effective.
Group B — Coach 2 · High Ball Control
1.
Coach 2 serves high, lofted balls from 15–20m toward a defender. Defender must set body shape before the ball arrives — side-on, goal-side of where they expect the ball to drop.
2.
No heading. Ball must be brought to feet or chest under control. Player then passes to the coach and repeats.
3.
Progress to: serve ball with a passive runner approaching from behind — defender must control despite contact pressure.
4.
Key question: "where were your feet before the ball arrived — were you goal-side?"
Why no heading: heading a long ball under pressure with poor body shape is how it gets nodded on to an attacker. Bringing the ball to feet or chest first is the safer, more controlled technique — and it's what was missing at the weekend.
Objective
Introduce the delay principle physically and technically. Group A builds the habit of arriving under control and jockeying rather than lunging — the 5-second rule removes the choice. Group B builds the technical foundation for dealing with the specific type of ball that caused the most damage at the weekend: the high, direct long ball into the defensive line.
Coaching Points — Group A (Delay)
Sprint to 2m — then slow everything down
The approach must be fast — closing distance quickly takes away the attacker's time. But arriving out of control is useless. At 2m, the defender shifts to a low jockey: bent knees, weight on toes, body side-on. Sprint → jockey is the two-step movement. Cue: "fast feet to close, slow feet to delay."
Delay
Show the attacker one direction — channel them
The delay is not passive standing — the defender's body angle actively channels the attacker toward the area of least danger (outside, away from goal, toward cover). Defenders who stand square give the attacker a free choice. The body angle is a decision, not a default.
Delay
Wait for the trigger — don't invent it
After 5 seconds of delay, the tackle attempt comes only on a clear trigger: heavy touch, dropped head, loss of balance. A defender who commits without a trigger gets beaten even after 10 seconds of good delay. The 5-second count removes the panic — what remains is patience.
Restraint
Coaching Points — Group B (High Ball)
Read the flight early — move before the ball peaks
A defender who moves after the ball has peaked is always reacting. A defender who reads the ball off the server's foot and starts moving as it rises is anticipating. The habit to build: "I know where this is going before it gets there." This is why the early body shape check matters more than the control itself.
Anticipation
Side-on body — see ball and runner simultaneously
Square-on defenders can see the ball or the runner but not both. Side-on — one shoulder toward goal, one toward the ball — gives the defender peripheral vision of both. This is the body shape that was absent for most of the goals conceded. It must become automatic before it works under match pressure.
Restraint
Progressions
A+
Group A: reduce the delay requirement to 3 seconds but add a second attacker who runs off-ball — defender must delay the ball-carrier AND stay aware of the runner. Replicates match conditions more closely.
B+
Group B: server now plays the ball to random positions (left, right, short) — defender cannot pre-set their position, must read each ball individually. Closer to match anticipation demands.
Regressions
A−
Widen channels to 12m — gives defenders more time to set body shape before the attacker arrives. Narrow back down once the jockey technique is established.
B−
Reduce serve height — lower, slower balls to establish the body shape habit before adding the difficulty of the fully lofted ball.
Group A — 1v1 Delay Channel (5-second rule)
5 channels · all running simultaneously · Ch3 shows coaching detail · defender must jockey for 5 full seconds before tackling · coach counts aloud
Group B — High Ball Body Shape
Wrong: square-on — cannot track ball and runner simultaneously. Correct: side-on — sees both, controls to feet, plays out.

Good Signs
Group A: defenders arriving fast but slowing to a controlled jockey — not charging
Group A: players surviving the full 5 seconds without lunging
Group B: body shape set before the ball peaks — anticipating not reacting
Group B: ball brought to feet, not headed aimlessly
Fix These
Group A: lunging before 5 seconds — call it immediately, award the point to attacker, repeat

Group B: ball consistently mis-controlled → lower serve height, establish body shape first before adding the full loft

Bridge — P1 to P2 · Merge all 22 · 60 seconds
Bring both groups together. Ask: "In the channel — what happened when you lunged early? What happened when you waited?" Take two answers. Then: "Now there are four of you defending together. Same rule: hold the line, don't step, let the attack come to you. The trigger to press only fires when you're all set."
Practice 2 — Wave: Unit Counter Defence
Wave Practice · All 22
4v4 Waves — Push High to Score, Survive the Space Behind
Attackers start with the ball · every 3rd attack = secret high ball · defenders score by dribbling to attack line
25MIN
Objective
Attacking team start with the ball and attack toward goal. Defending team score by dribbling the ball across the attacking team's start line — this reward encourages defenders to push higher and be aggressive, which naturally creates space in behind. One attacker's job is to read when a defender has pushed forward and immediately run into the gap they leave. Every third wave the coach secretly instructs the attacking team to play a high direct ball instead of building normally — defenders must deal with it without knowing it is coming.
⚽ Attackers Score
+1
Score a goal
+2
Goal from exploiting the space left by a defender who pushed forward
🛡 Defenders Score
+1
Any defender dribbles the ball across the attackers' start line
+1
Win the ball high in the attacking half (no dribble needed)
How It Works
1.
Attackers start with the ball and attack from the halfway line. Normal wave play.
2.
Defenders push up aggressively — if they win the ball, they can score by dribbling to the attackers' start line. This naturally pulls defenders out of position.
3.
One attacker (designated runner) watches for any defender who pushes too high and immediately runs into the space they vacate.
4.
Every 3rd wave (secret): Coach quietly tells attackers before the wave to play a high ball in behind instead of building. Defenders don't know it's coming — they must read and react as they would in a match.
⚠ Coach Only — Secret High Ball Protocol
Before waves 3, 6, 9, 12 etc., quietly tell the attacking group: "This one — play the high ball early." Do not let the defending team hear. The surprise replicates match conditions where defenders cannot anticipate the type of attack. After the wave, debrief the defending team: "What told you that was coming? Were you in the right shape to deal with it?" This develops anticipation rather than pattern recognition.
Rules
1.
Attackers start with ball at halfway. Defenders start at the trigger line (20m from goal). Wave starts on coach's signal.
2.
Defenders earn +1 by dribbling the ball across the attackers' start line — this requires a defender to push forward, leaving space in behind.
3.
One attacker is designated the runner — their sole job is to read when a defender has vacated space and make the run in behind.
4.
Every 3rd wave: attacking team play a high ball in behind at the first opportunity. Defending team are not told.
5.
Rotate waves — attacking and defending groups swap after every 4 waves.
Coaching Points
Runner: watch the defenders — not the ball
The designated runner's eyes are on the defensive line, not on where the ball is. The moment a defender steps forward, the run is made. The ball will find them if the timing is right. Cue: "you're hunting the gap, not the ball."
Exploit space
Defenders: when you go, someone must hold
Going to score on the dribble is a positive play — but the rest of the unit must hold their shape when one defender pushes. Two defenders pushing at the same time creates an unrecoverable gap. This is the cover-and-balance principle under competitive pressure.
Shape before press
High ball wave: drop, side-on, control — same as P1
The surprise high ball tests whether the P1 technique carries over under match pressure. Defenders who have internalised the body shape will deal with it; those who haven't will react square-on. The debrief after each high ball wave is the key coaching moment.
Anticipation
Progression
+
Increase to every 2nd wave being a high ball (still secret). As defenders adapt, increase frequency so they can no longer predict the pattern.
Regression
−
Tell defenders when a high ball wave is coming — remove the surprise until the correct body shape is consistent, then reintroduce the secret element.
P2 — Wave with Push-High Scoring & Secret High Ball
D1 pushes forward to score — leaves a gap. Runner reads it and exploits (+2). D2/D3/D4 hold shape. Every 3rd wave: coach secretly instructs attackers to play the high ball.
Watch ForPractice 2
Good Signs
Runner reacting immediately when a defender pushes — not waiting
Defenders communicating when one pushes — others hold shape
High ball waves: defenders dropping side-on, controlling to feet
Fix These
Two defenders pushing at same time — too much space left
Runner watching the ball, not the defensive line
High ball met square-on — remind of P1 body shape
If Not Working
Defenders not pushing at all → reduce to 3v4 so pushing is less risky, build confidence
Runner not reading gaps → designate them explicitly each wave, give them one job only
→
Bridge — P2 to P3 · 30 seconds
"Same idea in the game. Defenders — you score by stepping across the halfway line. But every time you do, you leave space. Attackers — hunt that space. Any direct ball in behind counts double."
Practice 3 — SSG: Defending Rewards
SSG · All 22 · Stay-On
7v7 + GK — Step Over Halfway to Score, Exploit the Gap
Quarter pitch · stay-on format · defender steps over halfway = goal · creates space behind
20MIN
Objective
7v7 + GK. A defending player scores a point for their team by dribbling or stepping across the opposing halfway line — the same mechanic as P2, now in a full game context. This incentivises defenders to push high, creating space in behind for the attacking team to exploit. The attacking team should look to play in behind any defending player who has advanced, particularly through direct balls. Stay-on format keeps intensity high.
🛡 Defending Team Scores
+1
Any defender dribbles or steps the ball across the halfway line into the opposition's half
+1
Normal goal at the attacking end
⚽ Attacking Team Scores
+1
Normal goal
+2
Goal from exploiting space left by a defender who stepped over halfway — direct ball in behind
How It Works
1.
Normal 7v7 game. Both teams attack and defend as normal.
2.
Defending players can score by dribbling or stepping with the ball across the halfway line. Team earns +1. This draws defenders forward and opens space in behind.
3.
Attacking team should look for the gap immediately when a defender pushes over halfway. Direct ball in behind that leads to a goal = +2.
4.
Team that concedes rotates off — waiting team comes on. Team that scores stays.
Format
3 teams of 7 · 1 GK per end
Rotation
Conceding team rotates off · waiting team on
Score line
Halfway — defenders dribble/step across
GKs
Permanent both ends
Final 5 min
Open — remove all conditions
Count
Defending team count "1–2–3–4–5" when holding shape
Rules
1.
Normal football — both teams attack and defend. Goalkeeper cannot step across halfway to score.
2.
A defending outfield player steps or dribbles the ball across the halfway line = +1 for their team. They must then return to their defensive shape immediately.
3.
The moment a defender steps over halfway, the attacking team may play a direct ball in behind for a +2 bonus if it leads to a goal.
4.
Defending team continues the verbal 5-count from P2 — counting aloud when holding shape. Reaching 5 earns possession.
5.
Final 5 minutes: no conditions, open game. Watch for retained delay and shape habits.
Debrief Questions
When a defender stepped over — did the attackers react quickly enough?
Players should identify the gap being there but not being exploited, or being exploited too slowly. The session trains this read — did it carry into the game?
Were the high balls from P2 easier to deal with in the game?
Connects P1 technical work through P2 and into the game. Players should notice improved body shape under match conditions.
One thing you'll do differently next match when you face a counter
Specific answer: drop first, hold the shape, side-on to the high ball. Not "defend better."
Progression
+
Add the secret high ball from P2 — coach signals to the attacking team periodically to play direct. Tests whether defenders can read and adjust without a warning.
If Conditions Cause Confusion
−
Remove the +2 bonus for exploiting the gap — just play with the defender step-over scoring rule. One condition at a time.
P3 — 7v7 + GK Stay-On · Defender Step-Over Scoring
D+ steps over halfway = +1. Leaves gap. Attacking team plays direct ball in behind = +2 if goal. Remaining defenders hold and 5-count. Team C waits to challenge winner.
Watch ForPractice 3
Good Signs
Attackers reacting instantly when defender steps over
Remaining defenders holding shape, not following the stepping player
Open final 5 min shows delay habits without the conditions
Fix These
Attackers slow to exploit the gap — cue them at rotation: "first look when they push is in behind"
Multiple defenders stepping — remind one goes, rest hold
If Not Working
Gap not being created → tell defending team one player must step over at least once per minute — forces the condition
Chaos → remove +2 bonus, keep only the step-over scoring rule
