Key Coaching Points — Group A
Drop before you engage on a direct ball
When a ball is played in behind, defenders must drop first and get goal-side before engaging. Stepping forward and missing leaves nothing behind.
Side-on body shape for the high ball
Turned side-on, the defender can see both the ball in the air and the runner's run simultaneously. Square-on means you can only see one.
Left channel — cover player always present when left CB presses
If the left defender steps to press, the central CB must slide left to cover. This was a recurring pattern in the last match — the habit needs to be automatic.
Key Coaching Points — Group B
Know your home position before you move
Every player has a "home" — where they recover to when the ball is in certain areas. The shadow drill builds this as muscle memory so it's automatic when coming on as a sub.
LWB & LCB — delay and channel, don't dive in
Close at pace, show the attacker inside toward cover. The first goal came from this exact area. These players need deliberate reps, not match exposure, to build the correct habit.

+
Add GK to Group A — organises the defensive unit with verbal calls ("drop!", "hold!") on each counter. Rehearses the communication that was absent vs Ekero.

Practice 2 — Finishing Under Movement
Finishing · 10 Players
Edge of Box Feed → Set → Shoot
3 stations of 3 + 1 GK · stations take turns · shooter rotates · 25 min
25MIN
Objective
Three stations of 3 players each on the edge of the penalty box. One player feeds from the edge, one player sets the ball up just inside the box, and one player runs onto the set to shoot on the move. After shooting, the shooter collects their ball and rejoins the back of their station queue. The setter becomes the next shooter. Stations take turns so the GK has time to reset between each shot.
How It Works — One Sequence
1
Feeder plays ball from the edge of the box into the Setter who is just inside the box
2
Setter lays the ball into the path of the Shooter who is running in from outside the box
3
Shooter arrives at the ball on the move and shoots — must not stop before striking
4
Shooter collects their ball, returns to back of queue · Setter becomes next Shooter · Feeder becomes next Setter
↻
Stations take turns — Station A shoots, then B, then C, then A again. GK resets between each shot. Coach calls "next station!" to keep the pace up.

Rules

2.

Key Coaching Points
Power comes from the run, not the stance
A player who stops before shooting loses all momentum. A moving approach transfers body weight into the ball — that's where the power comes from. Cue: "arrive at the ball, don't wait for it."
Standing foot next to the ball — not behind it
The non-kicking foot landing level with the ball allows hip rotation through the contact. If it lands behind the ball the shot is scooped and weak. Check this individually — it's the most common power problem.
Setter: lay off into the run — not to the feet
The set must go where the shooter is running, not where they are standing. If the shooter has to slow down to receive, the momentum for the shot is already lost.
Progression
+
Add a passive defender between the setter and the goal — shooter must commit to a direction before firing. Adds a decision layer without turning it into a competition.
If Struggling
−
Remove the moving rule for 3 minutes — let players find the set-and-shoot pattern first, then reintroduce the movement requirement once the sequence is understood.

Practice 3 — Conditioned 9v9 + GK
Full Integration · Match Conditions
Conditioned 9v9 + GK — All Session Themes Live
Full group · quarter pitch · 2 conditions · self-accountable · 20 min
20MIN
Objective
9v9 + GK on the quarter pitch. Two conditions only — one for finishing, one for defensive accountability. Both address the specific Ekero failures. No freeze coaching for first 15 minutes. Final 5 minutes open. The self-accountability rule on counter-attacks builds self-awareness after defensive breakdowns — fault is identified by the players themselves, not assigned by the coach.
Condition 1 — Finishing
A goal only counts if the scorer was moving when they struck the ball. Stationary shots that go in do not count — play restarts from the GK. No exceptions. This enforces the P2 habit directly into the game.
Condition 2 — Counter Accountability
If a counter-attack goal is conceded, play stops for 30 seconds. The defending team identifies who was responsible and why — no coach input. The analysis must be specific: "D-L didn't drop, the channel opened." Then restart. Player-led accountability, no blame from the coaching staff.
Left Channel Condition (bonus · optional)
If the coach observes three consecutive left-channel bypasses without a challenge, they call "left channel!" — the team must reshuffle and the next left-channel bypass counts double for the attacking team. This creates a consequence without singling out individual players. It creates accountability without singling anyone out in front of the group.
