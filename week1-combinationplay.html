Response to UEFA B Tutor Criteria — Session Adaptations
Tutor Feedback Four criteria reviewed — recommendations and session changes below
Criterion 1
Effective link from technical practice to game-realistic scenarios
The original P1→P2 transition lacked an explicit link. Added a bridge moment between practices: a 60-second coach-led freeze connecting the give & go seen in P1 to what players will now execute at pace and under more pressure in P2. P3 (conditioned match) then removes all structure so players apply learning freely — replicating 11v11 conditions. The combination rule (2+ touches) carries through all three practices as a consistent thread.
Change Made — Bridge Added
Criterion 2
Big picture — coach across units, affect players on/around/away from the ball
P2 (Wave Attack) already works across attacking and defensive units simultaneously — but the original plan focused only on attackers. Added explicit coaching notes for the defending unit behaviour and the CF progression's pressing role. The intervention strip in P2 explicitly identifies on-ball, around-ball, and off-ball players so the coach can consciously move between units during the practice. P3 (conditioned match) requires the coach to consciously observe all three units at once.
Change Made — Dual-Unit Notes Added
Criterion 3
Practice management — managing opposition to create appropriate challenge
In P2, the defending team's behaviour must be managed in three stages: (1) first 3 minutes — defend passively, no counter-attack allowed, so attackers get clean reps to understand the structure; (2) minutes 4–12 — counter-attack introduced, defenders at full intensity; (3) CF progression — defending team's persistent attacker presses high immediately, creating a realistic game-speed challenge. Management notes added to P2 explicitly to guide the coach on each stage.
Change Made — 3-Stage Mgmt Added
Criterion 4
Coaching interventions — instruction vs guided discovery balance
An intervention spectrum has been added to P2 and P3. It divides moments into three columns: when to freeze & instruct (shape is wrong, unsafe), when to use questioning without stopping (player made a decision — ask "what did you see?"), and when to let play run (combination attempted even if it failed — learning through consequence). The Problems & Solutions section specifies the intervention type for each problem. This directly addresses the instruction vs guided discovery balance your tutor flagged.

Practice 1 — Transfer Through Midfield
Technical-Tactical
3v3 + 2 Bounce Players — Transfer Through Midfield
Possession · Combination · Directional · All 14 Players
20–25MIN
Objective
Transfer the ball from outside bounce player A to outside bounce player B, using at least two inside players before the final pass. Both teams score the same way. 1 point per successful A-to-B transfer. The team currently in possession uses the two bounce players; on turnover the new possession team immediately uses them.
Area
20m × 15m
Inside
3v3 (6 players)
Outside
2 neutral bounce players
Waiting
GK + 5 rotate every 5 min
Touches
Free (encourage 1–2)
Min. touches
2 inside players before point
Rules
1.
Outside players are neutral — they serve whichever team has possession.
2.
Ball must touch at least 2 inside players before the scoring pass to the far outside player.
3.
Outside players cannot pass directly to each other — ball must re-enter the inside area.
4.
On turnover the new team may immediately reverse the transfer direction to score.
Coaching Points
One-two plays & wall passes — look for this first
Pass to teammate and immediately move into space for the return. The outside player is the ideal wall — use them, spin, receive back and penetrate.
Movement on, around and away from the ball
Three types: towards (short option), away (drag a defender to open space), diagonal (creates a passing lane and a third-man option at the same time).
Timing of the pass — into the run, not to the feet
Pass arrives as the receiver enters the space. Too early = intercepted. Too late = receiver has stopped. Cue: "pass to where they're going."
Three ways to penetrate: through · around · over
Through: split pass beating the line. Around: use bounce player wide, then switch to far end. Over: chip to the far bounce player when ground passes are blocked.
Progressions
P1
2-touch limit inside area — forces faster decision-making and combination tempo.
P2
All 3 inside players must touch — forces active third-man movement and off-ball runs.
P3
Coach names the combination ("one-two!", "third man!") — point only counts if the named combination was used.
Regressions
R1
Reduce to 1 inside touch required — simplify if players can't retain possession at all.
R2
Jogging defenders for first 2 minutes — introduce pressure gradually as players learn the structure.
P1 — Transfer Through Midfield (3v3 + 2 Bounce)
Success IndicatorsWhat to look for during Practice 1
Working
Players move immediately after passing
Give & go patterns emerging naturally
1 point roughly every 60–90 seconds
Verbal communication between players
Needs Attention
Play stalls at bounce player — no inside movement
One player dominating touches
Square/backward passes dominating under pressure

Problems & SolutionsFix without stopping play where possible
Players skip 2-touch rule to score immediately
Call "doesn't count!" instantly and give possession to defenders. Consequence is the most effective tool — no lecture needed.
Ball carrier holds too long — combination never starts
Call "one touch option!" without stopping. After the sequence: "where was the wall pass?" — guided discovery over instruction.
Players cluster around the ball — no movement away
Cue: "spread!" during play. If it persists: pause, ask "if three of you are here where does the pass go?" Then replay immediately.
Transitions chaotic when possession changes
Call "your ball, transfer!" the instant possession switches. Cues the new team to immediately look for the scoring pass. No stopping needed.
→
Bridge — Connecting P1 to P2 (60 seconds, do not skip)
Before moving to Practice 2, briefly gather players and ask: "What combination did you use most often to beat the defender and get the transfer done?" Let two or three players answer. Then say: "In the next practice you'll do exactly that — but now you're attacking a real goal at pace. The one-two you just used will open the door. Same idea, higher stakes, more space." This explicit verbal link is the critical pedagogical step connecting the technical exercise to the game-realistic scenario — and it satisfies Criterion 1 of your tutor's framework.
Practice 2 — Wave Attack 3v3
Big Picture — Transition
Wave Attack 3v3 — Half Line to Edge of Box
Attacking Transition · Counter-Attack · Dual Unit · All 14 Players
25–30MIN
Objective
Attack the opposite goal at pace using combination play. Score by shooting after at least 2 players have touched the ball. If the defence regains possession they can counter-attack immediately in the opposite direction. The practice creates a continuous wave of attacks from both ends, simulating transition and counter-attack in real match conditions.
Area
Half line → edge of box
Goals
Full goals both ends
Format
3v3 + GK both ends
Players waiting
Groups of 3 behind each goal
Min. touches
2 players before shot
Rotation
After score or stop — see rules
Rules
1.
Attacking 3 start behind their goal and sprint forward to attack the opposite goal at pace when the coach calls "go!" or on the coach's signal.
2.
Defending 3 start behind their goal. They defend and cannot advance past halfway until the attacking team cross it.
3.
If attackers score: they exit pitch to the side and walk back to their starting end. Next group attacks immediately.
4.
If defenders win possession: they counter-attack immediately toward the opposite goal. The attacking team must now defend.
5.
The end that started the previous attack defends the next wave. The cycle is continuous.
6.
Minimum 2 players must touch the ball before a shot — direct shot from the first touch of the first attacker does not count.
Practice Management — 3-Stage Opposition Challenge (Criterion 3)
Stage 1 (first 3–4 min): Defenders defend only — no counter-attack permitted. Give attackers clean reps to understand the structure, the combination rule, and the pace of the practice before adding defensive complexity.
Stage 2 (min 4–20): Full rules — defenders counter-attack on possession win. Raise intensity. This is the main body of the practice.
Stage 3 (CF Progression): Introduce persistent CF and pressing attacker — see below. Manage the pressing attacker's intensity — start at 50% press, then full press once attacking pairs understand the structure.
Coaching Intervention Spectrum — Instruction vs Guided Discovery (Criterion 4)
Freeze & Instruct
Support angle is completely wrong — ball carrier has zero options
Player shoots without 2-touch rule — stop, reset, explain consequence
Dangerous play or safety issue
Question Without Stopping
After a lost ball: "what did you see before you passed?"
After a failed combination: "when would have been a better moment to play that?"
After a good move: "what made that work?" — reinforce the principle
Let Play Run
Combination attempted but intercepted — player is learning through consequence
Player makes unexpected creative decision — allow expression
Practice is flowing with good pace — do not interrupt
Multi-Unit Coaching Focus — On/Around/Away from Ball (Criterion 2)
On the ball: first touch direction of the carrier — do they face forward or turn backwards? Quality of combination pass.
Around the ball: supporting attacker angle and distance — 45° support, close enough to receive but not too close to be marked. Are they showing for the give & go?
Away from the ball: third attacker's movement — timed run in behind, creating the third-man option. Do they move before the ball arrives at the support player?
Defensive unit: are defenders tracking the third man? Are they pressing as a unit or individually? Coach the defending 3 as actively as the attacking 3.
Coaching Points
Attack at pace — urgency from first touch
The moment they receive the ball to start the wave, the mentality is "forward." Every touch should advance the attack. Cue: "run at them, not around them."
First player sets the direction — others react
The carrier decides: go inside, go outside, or play the one-two. The other two read the decision and adjust their runs accordingly — not a scripted pattern but a read-and-react.
Create the 2v1 before the shot opportunity
The combination is a means to an end — use the give & go or the third-man run to create a numerical advantage that leads directly to a shooting position. Don't combine for its own sake.
Defend as a unit — press together, drop together
Defenders must communicate and press as a coordinated 3 — one presses, two cover. Isolated pressing creates easy combinations for the attackers. The defensive coaching is as important as the attacking coaching.
Progression — Persistent CF + Pressing Attacker
CF
Format changes to 2+CF vs 2+pressing attacker. Each team has a persistent centre forward who stays on the pitch. Attacking team: 2 players attack from behind goal, CF stays in the playing area as a link player (bounce passes only — CF cannot shoot). Defending team: their persistent attacker immediately presses the 2 attackers high to deny the direct pass into the CF. This mirrors a 4-3-3 striker receiving a ball under pressure from a holding midfielder — the combination principle is identical to P1 but now at full pace against a high press.
MGT
Start the pressing attacker at 50% intensity so the attacking pairs can experience the CF combination at least twice before full press is applied. Escalate to full press after 3–4 minutes.
Regressions
R1
Remove the counter-attack — defenders only defend for the first 3 minutes while structure is being learned.
R2
Reduce to 3v2 (remove one defender) — give attackers a numerical advantage to build confidence and reps before going to equal numbers.

Base 3v3 wave · attackers enter from behind goal · defend at halfway line

Success IndicatorsWhat to look for during Practice 2
Working
Attackers arrive at speed with urgency — no slow walk-in
Combinations recognisable — give & go or third-man visibly used
Counter-attacks happening quickly when defenders win ball
CF progression: CF receiving, laying off, combining quickly
Defenders communicating and pressing as a coordinated unit
Needs Attention
Attackers walking in — no urgency at the start of the wave
Direct shots without 2-touch rule being met
CF holding the ball too long — not laying off quickly
Defenders pressing individually rather than as a unit
Not Working — Act Now
No successful attacks in 4+ attempts → use R2 (3v2)
CF confusion on role → briefly clarify: "bounce only, no shot"
High press causing complete breakdown → reduce to 50% press
Problems & SolutionsIn-practice interventions for Practice 2
Attackers go too early — no combination, straight shot
Call "doesn't count" immediately. Don't stop. After the rep: "what combination gave you the 2v1?" — question without stopping, guided discovery.
Defenders just watching — not pressing as a unit
This is a coaching moment for the defensive unit. Brief freeze: "show me — where are you three?" Reposition as a unit. Replay. One instruction, not a lecture.
CF receiving and holding ball — not laying off
Call "one touch CF!" as a live cue. After the rep, ask the CF: "what were your options when the ball arrived?" Guided discovery — let them identify the lay-off.
High press completely preventing any combination
Temporarily reduce pressing attacker to 50% intensity. Coach the attackers: "the press is blocking the direct pass — what's your alternative?" Then restore full press.
→
Bridge — Connecting P2 to P3 (60 seconds)
Ask: "In the wave game, which combination opened the goal for you — the one-two with the CF or the third-man run?" Take two answers. Then: "Now we play a proper game. You'll have more players, more space, and both teams trying to score. But the combinations are still your primary tool. When you see the opportunity — use it." This completes the technical→unit→game arc and opens P3 with the players' own language and experience.
Practice 3 — Conditioned Match
Full Integration — Expression
Conditioned Match — 7v6+GK · Combination to Goal
Game-Realistic · All Principles Live · 14 Players
25–30MIN
Objective
Play a conditioned match on the full half pitch. The single condition mirrors the whole session: a goal only counts if at least 2 players have touched the ball before the shot is taken. All combinations from P1 and P2 are now available simultaneously and freely chosen by players. No freeze coaching for the first 20 minutes. The final 10 minutes is completely open — no conditions, all goals count.
Format
7v6+GK
Pitch
Full half pitch
Condition
2 players touch before shot
Defending goal
2 mini-goals on halfway
Coaching
Observe first 20 min; open last 10
Subs
Rolling — no player out >4 min
Rules
1.
Goal only counts if 2 or more players touch ball before the shot — direct shots from first touch do not count.
2.
Defending team score by winning possession and playing into 2 mini-goals on halfway — replicating transition to attack.
3.
No freeze coaching for first 20 minutes — coaches observe, question after sequences, call combinations aloud positively.
4.
Final 10 minutes: open game — no condition, any goal counts. Players express freely.
5.
End-of-session debrief: coach asks each player to name one combination they used and one they want to improve.
Coaching Intervention Spectrum — P3 (Criterion 4)
Freeze & Instruct
Shape is broken across two or three players — systemic problem requiring a clear correction
Combination rule consistently ignored — one brief, sharp reset
Question Without Stopping
After lost ball: "what did you see?" or "what was the alternative?"
After good combination: "what made that possible?" — reinforce verbally
Acknowledge creativity: name it loudly — "that's a third-man run!"
Let Play Run
Players are experimenting freely — do not interrupt flow
Unexpected combination or creative solution — let it play out
Good pace and engagement — silence is the best tool
Coaching Points
Combination play creates space — use it to shoot
The combination is the tool. The shot is the goal. Don't combine indefinitely — recognise when the combination has created the shooting opportunity and take it.
Read the game — choose the right combination
There is no single right answer. A give & go suits a tight space. A third-man run suits a defender playing flat. Positional rotation suits a compact block. Players should recognise the situation and select.
Express yourself — creativity is celebrated
If something unexpected works, name it and praise it. The condition creates structure; within that structure there is complete creative freedom. A failed creative attempt is always better than no attempt.
Progressions
P1
Coach names a specific combination before each restart — players must attempt that combination in the next sequence. Builds intentional selection habit.
P2
Combination goal = 2pts; non-combination goal = 1pt. Creates incentive to attempt more complex patterns.
Regressions
R1
Remove the condition entirely — open match. Reintroduce condition after 5 minutes when players are comfortable.
End-of-Session Debrief (5 min)
Gather players in a circle. Ask individually: "Name one combination you used today and one moment you'd do differently." This satisfies Criterion 4 (guided discovery — metacognitive awareness) and closes the session pedagogically. Coaches share one observation each — focused on movement and decision-making quality, not outcome.
