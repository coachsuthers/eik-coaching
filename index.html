The Context

This portfolio documents the practical coaching work completed as part of the UEFA B Licence programme. It covers a full competitive youth season with an under-15 squad at Essinge IK in Stockholm, working across attacking and defensive principles, unit defending concepts and session design methodology.

Every session plan here was used in a real training environment with real players. The designs, conditions and coaching points were adapted in response to match results, player ability and the evolving demands of the group across the season.

What's Documented

Each session includes a full practice plan with animated pitch diagrams, coaching points, progressions and regressions. Several sessions include post-session reflections written immediately after training — recording what worked, what was adapted mid-session and what carries forward to the following week.

The session thread runs from combination play and attacking principles through to a sustained focus on defensive organisation — unit shape, compactness, delay and restraint — driven by results and patterns observed across the competitive season.

02 — Coaching Philosophy
Principle 1
Game-Based Learning

Every practice is built around a competitive condition that mirrors a real match scenario. The scoring system creates the tactical problem — players discover the right behaviour through consequence, not instruction. The coach designs the game, not the answer.

Principle 2
Progressive Complexity

Each session builds on the one before it. Concepts introduced as functional drills become unit practices, then full game conditions. Players encounter the same idea at increasing levels of complexity — familiarity builds confidence, and confidence enables risk-taking.

Principle 3
Honest Reflection

Sessions are planned in detail but adapted freely. What matters is what actually happened — the mid-session changes, the moments that didn't work, the players who needed a different approach. Coaching is not a performance. It is a response.

P.01Decisions made under pressure are more durable than habits taught under instruction
P.02A practice that looks chaotic may be working exactly as intended
P.03The most important coaching often happens in the 60 seconds between practices
P.04Ability splits improve outcomes for stronger players but carry a real cost for weaker ones — manage this actively
P.05Defensive principles are best introduced through attacking problems: give the attacking team the advantage, and defenders find the solution
P.06A deferred practice is not a failure — it is the opening of the following week
